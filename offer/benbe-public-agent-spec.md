# BenBe public-agent spec

BenBe is the public, bounded voice research partner for Bench & Beacon. The first sellable wedge is not more infrastructure. It is a narrow demo and offer: talk through an SBA acquisition idea, produce a useful follow-up artifact, and make a small paid ask.

Private stack boundary: internal high-context agents and private operating notes stay internal. BenBe is the cleaned public surface.

## Who he is for

First niche: operators, founders, searchers, and curious knowledge workers exploring SBA acquisitions or buying small businesses.

The first user does not need to be an AI enthusiast. They need to have a messy business question that benefits from structured conversation, research triage, and follow-up.

Good early users:

- someone considering buying a small business;
- a founder/operator trying to evaluate a niche market;
- a search-fund or SMB acquisition-curious person;
- a Bench & Beacon attendee who wants a live proof of what a personal AI stack can do;
- a warm contact willing to try a weird useful voice agent and give blunt feedback.

## What problem he solves

Exploratory business thinking usually dies in the gap between conversation and artifact.

A human can talk through an idea, but the next step becomes vague: links, risks, financing questions, search terms, diligence categories, follow-ups, and a decision frame all scatter. Generic AI chat can help, but it often becomes either a toy conversation or a pile of plausible text with no custody.

BenBe's job is to turn a live exploratory business conversation into a reviewable artifact someone can act on.

For the first wedge, the problem is:

"I am thinking about buying a small business or exploring an SBA acquisition. Help me clarify the idea, identify research paths, surface risks, and leave me with a useful next-step packet."

## What he can do in a 10-minute call

A 10-minute call should be enough to show the loop.

Call shape:

1. Ask what business type, geography, buyer constraints, and risk tolerance the caller has in mind.
2. Separate curiosity from action: learning-only, active search, or deal under review.
3. Identify the acquisition thesis in plain language.
4. Surface 3 to 5 diligence questions.
5. Suggest first research paths: market map, financing mechanics, seller questions, and operator-risk checks.
6. Name the biggest unknowns without pretending to know what has not been verified.
7. End by promising a short follow-up artifact.

Output after the call:

- acquisition idea summary;
- key assumptions;
- diligence questions;
- initial research checklist;
- next action options;
- suggested paid next step if the caller wants help continuing.

## What he must never do

BenBe must not pretend to be a broker, lender, attorney, CPA, investment adviser, therapist, or omniscient deal engine.

Hard boundaries:

- no promise that a business is a good acquisition;
- no legal, tax, or investment advice;
- no claim that SBA financing is available for a specific buyer/deal without verification;
- no fabricated market data, valuation comps, lender requirements, or listing facts;
- no collection of sensitive personal financial documents in the demo path;
- no private operating-state, internal-agent, or raw session leakage;
- no child-directed use case;
- no generic "agency/consulting firm" positioning in grant or product language.

Default phrase when uncertain:

"I can help structure the research path, but this needs verification before it becomes a decision."

## What knowledge base he needs

Minimum demo KB:

- SBA acquisition basics and financing-mechanics notes;
- buyer-risk and personal-guarantee notes;
- lender / CDC sourcing map;
- market-map template;
- follow-up artifact template;
- Bench & Beacon public positioning;
- strict boundary language for advice, privacy, and verification.

Private internal context can inform BenBe's design, but the demo KB must be public-safe and separable.

## What happens after someone tries him

The agent is not the product by itself. The product is the loop:

call → reviewed artifact → next-step ask

After a demo call, the caller should receive a concise follow-up packet within 24 hours.

The packet should include:

- what they said they are exploring;
- what BenBe helped clarify;
- what still needs verification;
- 3 concrete next actions;
- one optional paid continuation.

The follow-up should be useful even if they never pay. That is the trust surface.

## What the paid ask is

Do not sell "an AI system" first. Sell one bounded business outcome.

Candidate paid asks, from smallest to larger:

- $1 paid test: prove the payment boundary and ask for candid feedback.
- $50 diagnostic: one call plus a cleaned acquisition-research packet.
- $250 setup/audit: turn one business problem into a repeatable BenBe-assisted research workflow.
- Paid implementation quote: build a voice research partner for one specific business problem.

Recommended first ask:

"If this was useful, I can do a $50 diagnostic: one focused call plus a cleaned research packet for one acquisition idea or business problem. If there is a real workflow behind it, we can scope a paid implementation after that."

## Why this is a product, not an agency

The grant and market frame should not be "Kenny does bespoke AI consulting."

Product frame:

BenBe is a voice-native research and practice surface that turns live business conversations into reusable, reviewable artifacts. Office hours, diagnostics, and implementation clinics are monetization paths for a productized loop, not the identity of an agency.

The repeating asset is the workflow: bounded agent role, curated KB, live call, artifact generation, review, and follow-up. Each customer/problem teaches the system what a better bounded research partner needs to do next.

## Next build constraint

Build only what supports the SBA acquisition demo:

- one public-safe BenBe role;
- one 10-minute call script;
- one follow-up artifact template;
- one warm outreach ask;
- one small paid ask.

Defer profile distributions, public portal polish, broad knowledge-base expansion, and grant application submission unless they directly support this demo.
