# Bench & Beacon YouTube Project

## Canonical Direction

Bench & Beacon should not launch as a generic YouTube channel. It should launch as a weekly live-project dispatch:

`one Friday video -> two derivative posts -> followups -> next live project`

The video is the main artifact. The writing, clips, and posts come downstream from the same weekly idea.

Business constraint:

The channel exists to create public proof for the Bench & Beacon first-dollar path before July 13. Do not let channel polish displace concrete use cases, offer language, and the smallest paid ask.

Default format:

- 12 to 18 minute produced video.
- On-camera hook or bookend.
- iA Presenter or Keynote deck.
- Screen/demo footage when useful.
- One concrete artifact shown on screen.
- Practical CTA tied to the personalized AI stack offer.

## Folder Map

- `launch-plan.md` - phased checklist from setup through first public video.
- `asset-inventory.md` - vetted usable assets, verification items, and explicit avoid list.
- `content-slate.md` - first episode, first 8 weeks, and backlog.
- `templates.md` - copy/paste-ready channel, episode, metadata, and production templates.
- `research/` - original GPT, Claude, and Grok outputs.

## Production Themes

Where all three models agree:

- The channel is feasible with existing gear and software.
- The first real bottleneck is setup tax: templates, folder structure, thumbnail system, recording workflow, and rehearsal.
- The first video should be based on the flywheel thesis or weekly briefing automation.
- Video should lead the weekly content system, not sit beside it.
- Use a clean lecture for the first flywheel video. Use live-capture plus trim for Ben B demos after the pilot.
- Put automation behind the cadence. Do not wire BenBe, Buffer, or Shorts automation before one strong public episode exists.

## Immediate Next Moves

1. Verify channel state in YouTube Studio: handle, phone verification, custom thumbnails, links, default audience, upload defaults.
2. Pick the canonical public domain for lower thirds and descriptions.
3. Build the minimum visual kit: avatar, banner, thumbnail master, deck master, lower third.
4. Record a private 60-second upload test with camera, mic, deck, screen capture, export, thumbnail, chapters, and captions.
5. Package and record the first public episode.
6. Attach the first episode to one concrete paid ask or use-case collection prompt.
